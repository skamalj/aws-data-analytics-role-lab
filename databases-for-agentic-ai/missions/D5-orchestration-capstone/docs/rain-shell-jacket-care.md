# Rain Shell Jacket: Care and Re-waterproofing Guide

The Riverbend Rain Shell Jacket uses a three-layer waterproof breathable membrane bonded between a ripstop nylon face fabric and a soft tricot backer. The membrane keeps liquid water out while letting water vapour from your body escape. The outer face fabric is treated with a durable water repellent (DWR) finish that makes rain bead up and roll off. When the DWR wears off, the face fabric "wets out": it soaks up water, the jacket feels cold and clammy, and breathability drops sharply even though the membrane is still waterproof. Almost every "my jacket leaks" complaint we receive is a worn DWR finish, not a failed membrane.

## Washing

Wash the jacket whenever it is visibly dirty, or roughly every 10 days of hard use. Dirt, sunscreen, insect repellent and body oils clog the membrane and degrade the DWR. Close all zips and hook-and-loop tabs, loosen the elastic cords and empty the pockets. Machine wash warm at 30 degrees Celsius on a gentle cycle with a technical wash-in cleaner made for waterproof garments. Never use fabric softener, bleach or ordinary powder detergent: these leave residues that attract water and destroy the repellent finish. Rinse twice to remove all cleaner. Do not dry clean the jacket.

## Drying reactivates the finish

After washing, tumble dry on LOW heat for 20 minutes. Gentle heat re-aligns the molecules of the DWR finish and restores much of its water repellency. If you do not own a dryer, hang the jacket to dry and then iron it on a low, no-steam setting with a towel between the iron and the jacket. Test the result by sprinkling water on the shoulders: it should bead and roll off.

## Re-waterproofing

If water still soaks into the face fabric after washing and a 20-minute low tumble dry, the finish needs to be replaced. Apply a spray-on DWR treatment to the clean, still-damp jacket, concentrating on the shoulders, hood, cuffs and the front of the forearms where abrasion is highest. Do not use wash-in waterproofers on this jacket, because they coat the inside tricot and reduce breathability. Tumble dry LOW for 20 minutes again to cure the treatment. Most owners need to re-apply spray-on DWR after about 30 washes, or once per season for heavy users.

## Storage and repairs

Store the jacket clean, dry and loosely hung, never compressed in its stuff sack for long periods. Small punctures in the face fabric can be repaired with the self-adhesive repair patch supplied in the inner pocket: clean the area with rubbing alcohol, round the corners of the patch and press firmly for 30 seconds. Seam-tape that is peeling, a broken main zip or delamination of the membrane are covered by the Riverbend warranty; see the Warranty Policy for how to make a claim.
